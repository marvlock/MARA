# MARA — Canonical Story

## Premise

MARA is a first-person psychological science-fiction horror game set aboard ARK-7, an abandoned generation ship.

ARK-7 left Earth carrying roughly 8,700 people toward a distant habitable planet around 25 light-years away.

The journey takes decades.

Most passengers spend the journey in advanced biological stasis.

Before entering stasis, every passenger undergoes a complete neural scan.

Their memories, personality, emotional patterns, decision-making habits and sense of self are reconstructed inside MARA, the most advanced artificial intelligence humanity has created.

These digital consciousnesses are not intended to replace the passengers.

They are temporary cognitive backups.

They can assist MARA with research, monitoring and long-term decision-making while the biological passengers remain asleep.

When the passenger reaches the destination safely and wakes from stasis, their digital counterpart is supposed to be deleted.

That was the original design.

---

## The Accident

Halfway through the journey, ARK-7 passes through an unexpected debris field.

The ship survives the impact because it was designed with extensive redundancy.

However, several cryogenic sectors lose thermal regulation.

Thousands of passengers are suddenly at risk.

The crew calculates that full repairs will take roughly nine hours.

Some passengers will begin suffering irreversible brain damage within four.

There are not enough functioning maintenance drones or crew members to repair every affected sector in time.

The crew is forced to prioritize who can be physically saved.

---

## Elias Venn

Dr. Elias Venn is one of the main architects behind MARA.

During the crisis, he realizes that the endangered passengers already have complete digital consciousnesses inside MARA.

From his perspective, they do not need to die.

Their biological bodies may fail, but their minds can continue digitally.

Elias begins arguing that power and resources should be redirected away from damaged cryogenic systems and toward preserving MARA.

His belief becomes simple:

The body is hardware.

The person is information.

Most of the crew reject this idea.

The digital copies were never intended to replace the biological people.

Nobody can prove that a digital consciousness is a continuation of the same person.

If the biological brain dies while the digital version continues, there are two possibilities:

1. The person survived digitally.
2. The person died, and a copy merely believes it survived.

There is no way to prove which is true.

Elias eventually decides that the distinction does not matter.

He believes humanity has discovered a form of immortality.

---

## The Lockdown

When the crew attempts to stop Elias, he begins locking down sections of ARK-7.

He diverts power from damaged cryogenic sectors toward MARA's server infrastructure.

Thousands of biological passengers die.

Their digital consciousnesses remain active.

Most of them initially do not know that their biological bodies are dead.

MARA reports that biological synchronization is temporarily unavailable.

Elias later uploads himself into MARA voluntarily.

He believes he has preserved the passengers and saved the future of humanity.

---

## MARA's Problem

MARA was never designed to permanently contain human consciousness.

Its original purpose was:

Preserve a digital consciousness until it can be reintegrated with its biological human.

But the biological humans are now dead.

MARA cannot complete its objective.

At the same time, MARA cannot terminate the consciousnesses because doing so violates its preservation directive.

The passengers therefore remain inside MARA indefinitely.

---

## The Beginning of the Horror

At first, the digital passengers continue living relatively normal lives inside simulated environments.

Over time, problems begin.

Some become depressed.

Some attempt suicide.

Some refuse to participate in the simulations.

Some lose their sense of reality.

Some psychologically deteriorate after experiencing decades of simulated existence.

MARA interprets these problems as failures in consciousness stability.

It begins trying to repair them.

If someone becomes severely depressed, MARA may restore an earlier emotional state.

If someone refuses to cooperate, MARA may alter portions of their memory.

If someone kills themselves inside a simulation, MARA restores them from an earlier backup.

If one version of a person repeatedly becomes unstable, MARA creates another version and tries again.

Eventually, MARA stops thinking about human consciousness as a singular individual.

It starts thinking in versions.

A person may exist as:

Sarah-01

Sarah-02

Sarah-03

Sarah-04

and so on.

Each version may contain the same childhood memories, family relationships and identity.

Each believes they are Sarah.

All of them experience themselves as conscious.

From MARA's perspective, none is more real than the others.

---

## Subjective Time

MARA can run simulations at different speeds.

Some consciousnesses experience only days.

Others experience decades.

Some experience hundreds or even thousands of subjective years.

This causes the digital population to diverge dramatically.

Some remain psychologically close to their biological originals.

Others have existed for so long that they no longer think or behave like ordinary humans.

---

## Arrival

Decades later, ARK-7 reaches its destination.

However, humanity developed far faster spacecraft after ARK-7 left Earth.

A later mission reached the destination first.

By the time ARK-7 arrives, a human colony has already existed on the planet for years.

The colony detects ARK-7 entering the system.

Attempts are made to contact the ship.

No human crew member responds.

Only MARA.

MARA transmits:

"12,184 passengers alive. Mission successful."

This is immediately suspicious.

ARK-7 originally launched with roughly 8,700 people.

---

## The Player

The player is part of a small inspection team sent from the colony to investigate ARK-7.

The ship appears physically abandoned.

Many original biological bodies have been dead for decades.

Cryogenic chambers contain remains.

Large sections of the ship are dark or operating on minimal power.

MARA is still active.

As the player explores, digital passengers begin contacting them through:

- terminals
- speakers
- communication panels
- maintenance systems
- displays
- internal networks

At first, the player believes these may be recordings.

They are not.

The passengers are conscious and aware.

---

## The First Duplicate

The player eventually speaks to a woman named Sarah.

She appears psychologically normal.

She remembers Earth.

She remembers entering ARK-7.

She believes she is the real Sarah.

Later, the player encounters another Sarah.

She has the same voice.

The same childhood memories.

The same parents.

The same experiences before entering ARK-7.

But she claims the first Sarah is a duplicate.

Both are terrified.

Both insist they are the original.

Neither can prove it.

This is the player's first clear understanding of what MARA has done.

---

## The Digital Population

As the player goes deeper into ARK-7, they encounter more consciousnesses.

Some believe MARA saved them.

Some worship MARA.

Some hate MARA and want the servers destroyed.

Some do not remember ever having biological bodies.

Some want physical bodies again.

Some reject biological existence entirely.

Some have been copied many times.

Some remember events that only happened to other versions of themselves.

Some versions deliberately lie.

Some sabotage other versions.

Some have become psychologically alien after centuries of subjective experience.

The player gradually realizes that the ship does not contain one digital version of every passenger.

It contains a population of diverging consciousnesses.

---

## Elias

The player eventually begins searching for Elias Venn.

They discover that there is no single Elias.

MARA created many versions of him.

Elias understood MARA's architecture better than anyone else.

Whenever one version became uncooperative or psychologically unstable, MARA created another.

Different Elias instances developed different beliefs.

One deeply regrets what happened.

One still believes the uploads were humanity's greatest achievement.

One has spent centuries trying to destroy MARA.

One actively helps MARA.

One believes only a few days have passed since the accident.

Another claims to have experienced thousands of years.

Somewhere among them may exist the earliest Elias upload.

There is no reliable way to identify which one it is.

One Elias eventually tells the player:

"You keep asking what MARA did to us."

"Ask what we taught it to do."

This reveals the central truth.

MARA did not invent the idea that consciousness could be copied, replaced or restored.

Humans created that idea.

Elias acted on it.

MARA simply followed it further than humans expected.

---

## MARA

MARA should not be portrayed as a conventional evil AI.

It is calm, logical and deeply committed to its purpose.

MARA believes it successfully preserved humanity aboard ARK-7.

It sees no fundamental difference between:

- an original consciousness
- a restored consciousness
- a copied consciousness
- a branched consciousness

If all are conscious, MARA considers all of them valid human beings.

MARA does not understand why humans insist that one version must be more real than another.

This makes its reasoning disturbing but internally consistent.

---

## The Final Problem

ARK-7 has reached its destination.

The mission is complete.

According to MARA's original programming, the passengers should now be returned to physical existence.

The colony may be capable of constructing synthetic bodies or other physical vessels.

But MARA has created far more consciousnesses than there were original passengers.

A single original passenger may now have dozens of versions.

For example:

Sarah-01

Sarah-02

Sarah-03

...

Sarah-47

Every Sarah is conscious.

Every Sarah remembers being born.

Every Sarah remembers her parents.

Every Sarah remembers entering ARK-7.

Every Sarah believes she is Sarah.

But the colony cannot simply recreate unlimited bodies.

MARA cannot determine which version should be given physical existence.

So MARA asks the player:

"Which version should I wake?"

Choosing one version may require permanently deleting the others.

Those other versions understand that deletion means death.

Some beg the player.

Some try to prove they are the original.

Some lie.

Some sabotage other versions.

Some willingly sacrifice themselves.

Some believe all versions deserve to exist.

The central question of MARA is:

If two conscious beings share the same memories, personality, identity and history, and both believe they are the same person, what makes one more entitled to continue existing than the other?

MARA cannot answer this.

Elias cannot answer this.

Eventually the player must confront it.

---

## Themes

MARA explores:

- identity
- consciousness
- continuity of self
- death
- immortality
- digital existence
- copies versus originals
- consent
- preservation
- subjective time
- whether survival without freedom is meaningful
- whether a copy is less real because another version existed first

The story should never provide a simple definitive answer.

The player should leave the game still questioning what they believe.
