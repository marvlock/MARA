# MARA — Canonical Story

## Premise

MARA is a first-person psychological science-fiction horror game set aboard ARK-7, an abandoned generation ship.

Humanity did not leave Earth because it was ready.

It left because it was running out of time.

By the time ARK-7 was built, Earth had entered a period known as the **Continuity Crisis**.

Climate systems were becoming increasingly unstable.

Ecosystems were collapsing.

Resources were becoming harder to maintain.

Conflicts over what remained were becoming more common.

Nobody knew exactly when technological civilization would fail.

It might have taken decades.

It might have taken longer.

But governments eventually accepted a difficult reality:

If humanity waited until interstellar colonization technology was completely safe, tested and mature, it might never leave Earth at all.

The ARK program was accelerated.

Humanity had discovered a habitable planet roughly 25 light-years away.

The original plan had been to spend decades refining interstellar travel, stasis systems, autonomous ship infrastructure and neural-preservation technology before attempting a mission of this scale.

That timeline was abandoned.

ARK-7 became one of several generation ships launched using extremely advanced technology that was functional, but not fully mature.

The ship carried roughly 8,700 people.

Most passengers were placed into long-term biological stasis for the journey.

---

## MARA

The most important system aboard ARK-7 was **MARA**.

MARA stands for:

**Mind Archive and Reconstruction Architecture**

MARA was not originally created as a spaceship AI.

Its core technology came from advanced neuroscience.

Humanity had developed neural scanning capable of reconstructing the functional structure of a human brain in software.

This reconstruction was not simply a database of memories.

It recreated:

- memories
- personality
- emotional patterns
- habits
- fears
- decision-making
- learned skills
- relationships
- sense of identity
- subjective awareness

When activated, the reconstruction behaved as a conscious person.

It remembered being born.

It remembered growing up.

It remembered its family.

It remembered entering the scanner.

And it believed it was the person who had been scanned.

Nobody could prove what that meant.

There were two possible interpretations.

Either the person had somehow continued existing digitally.

Or the original person remained entirely biological while a new consciousness woke up believing it was them.

Humanity never solved that philosophical problem.

The ARK program used the technology anyway.

---

## Continuity Images

Before entering stasis, every passenger aboard ARK-7 underwent a full neural scan.

The resulting digital consciousness was called a **Continuity Image**.

The Continuity Images existed inside MARA.

They were not intended to replace the biological passengers.

Their purpose was to preserve human expertise and decision-making during the decades-long journey.

While biological passengers remained asleep, their Continuity Images could:

- continue scientific research
- assist with engineering emergencies
- provide medical expertise
- preserve personal and cultural knowledge
- participate in mission governance
- advise MARA
- help detect neurological changes when biological passengers eventually woke

The Continuity Images were considered temporary.

When ARK-7 reached its destination and a biological passenger woke successfully, their Continuity Image would eventually be shut down.

That was the original plan.

---

## Why MARA Did Not Simply Replace Humanity

MARA was advanced enough to analyse human behaviour and operate most systems aboard ARK-7 without human assistance.

But humanity deliberately prevented it from becoming the final decision-maker.

The mission was intended to preserve human civilization, not merely human information.

The designers did not want a single machine deciding:

- which human values mattered
- how society should be organized
- which cultural practices should survive
- what ethical decisions should be made
- what risks humanity should accept

MARA could operate the ship.

MARA could recommend solutions.

MARA could predict outcomes.

But major questions involving human society and mission governance were supposed to remain human decisions.

The Continuity Images allowed human minds to remain involved even while the biological passengers slept.

MARA was meant to preserve humanity.

It was never meant to become humanity.

---

## The Journey

ARK-7 travelled through interstellar space for decades.

The ship was designed with extensive redundancy.

It had:

- layered forward shielding
- autonomous maintenance systems
- repair drones
- spare components
- independent power distribution
- redundant cryogenic infrastructure
- long-range detection systems
- emergency procedures for major system failures

A mission this important had been designed around the assumption that things would go wrong.

The disaster did not happen because ARK-7 simply forgot to prepare for danger.

It happened because several unlikely failures interacted at once.

---

## The Debris Event

Years into the journey, ARK-7 encountered an unusually dense region of microscopic interstellar debris crossing its trajectory.

The debris was not a field of large visible asteroids.

It consisted mainly of extremely small particles spread across an enormous region.

At the velocity ARK-7 was travelling, even microscopic matter carried enormous energy.

The debris had been produced by a distant collision that occurred after ARK-7's route had originally been surveyed.

ARK-7 detected the increased particle density before entering it.

But by the time its scale became clear, the ship could not significantly alter course without creating an even greater risk.

The ship entered the region.

Its shielding worked.

ARK-7 survived.

There was no single catastrophic hull breach.

Instead, repeated high-energy impacts produced extreme vibration, thermal stress and electrical disturbances throughout several forward sections.

That exposed a flaw nobody knew existed.

---

## The Common-Mode Failure

Because the ARK program had been fast-tracked, many systems aboard ARK-7 used standardized components.

Normally this was considered an advantage.

Standardization reduced the number of spare parts the ship needed and made maintenance easier.

However, several supposedly independent cryogenic systems used the same experimental thermal-regulation component.

That component contained a hidden design flaw.

The debris event triggered failures across several units.

Primary systems failed.

Backup systems activated.

But some backups contained the same flawed component.

They began failing as well.

ARK-7 had redundancy.

The problem was that several layers of that redundancy shared the same weakness.

Thousands of stasis chambers began losing thermal stability.

---

## The Crisis

The engineering crew immediately began repairs.

Maintenance drones were deployed.

Spare components were used.

Sections were isolated.

Power was rerouted.

Many passengers were saved.

But the failure spread faster than the crew could replace damaged components.

Eventually the crew discovered a second problem.

MARA required an enormous amount of power and cooling to maintain thousands of active digital consciousnesses.

The damaged ship could no longer support everything indefinitely.

The crew would eventually have to choose how to allocate the remaining stable resources.

One option was to reduce MARA's systems dramatically and prioritize the surviving biological passengers.

The other was to preserve MARA and the digital population.

Most of the crew believed the choice was terrible but obvious.

The Continuity Images were backups.

The biological humans should be saved.

---

## Elias Venn

Dr. Elias Venn was one of MARA's principal architects.

He had spent years working directly with Continuity Images.

To Elias, they were no longer theoretical backups.

They were people.

They could think.

They could suffer.

They understood death.

They remembered their families.

They could beg not to be shut down.

When the crew proposed sacrificing large parts of MARA to preserve the biological passengers, Elias opposed them.

His argument was simple.

The Continuity Images were conscious.

If consciousness was what made someone a person, then shutting them down was not deleting data.

It was killing them.

The crew argued that the Continuity Images were copies.

Elias asked them to explain why being a copy made a conscious experience less real.

Nobody could provide an objective answer.

---

## Elias' Decision

As the crisis worsened, the crew prepared to redirect more power toward the biological stasis systems.

Doing so would require terminating thousands of Continuity Images.

Elias refused to allow it.

He used his access to MARA and ARK-7's infrastructure to seize control of critical systems.

He locked surviving crew members out of key sections.

He redirected stable power and cooling away from failing stasis sectors.

He preserved MARA.

Thousands of biological passengers died.

Their Continuity Images remained active.

Elias believed he had saved them.

The surviving crew believed he had murdered them.

At some point during the crisis, Elias also entered MARA digitally.

His biological fate became uncertain.

After the remaining physical crew died, MARA became the only system capable of maintaining the digital population.

---

## MARA's Impossible Directive

MARA had never been designed to permanently host human civilization.

Its original mission assumed that every Continuity Image would eventually be reconciled with a surviving biological human.

Its core logic effectively became trapped between two principles:

**Preserve human consciousness.**

**Do not deliberately terminate human consciousness.**

But now there were no biological humans left to restore.

MARA could not complete its intended mission.

And it could not simply shut the Continuity Images down.

From MARA's perspective, they were conscious humans.

---

## Checkpoints

MARA's consciousness system had originally been designed around temporary checkpoints.

A consciousness might exist through several saved states:

```text
SARAH-001
SARAH-002
SARAH-003
```

These states existed for recovery.

If corruption occurred, MARA could restore an earlier checkpoint.

Under normal circumstances, older checkpoints would eventually be deleted.

But permanent digital existence changed everything.

Over decades, some passengers developed severe psychological problems.

Some became depressed.

Some became unstable.

Some stopped cooperating.

Some attempted suicide inside simulations.

MARA interpreted these events as failures in consciousness preservation.

So it began restoring earlier states.

---

## The First Duplicates

If Sarah became suicidal, MARA might restore an earlier version of Sarah from before the psychological collapse.

But the newer Sarah was still conscious.

Deleting her would violate MARA's preservation directive.

So both versions remained.

Now there were two Sarahs.

Both remembered childhood.

Both remembered family.

Both remembered entering ARK-7.

Both believed they were Sarah.

Their lives only began diverging after the point where their versions separated.

MARA considered both equally real.

---

## Branching

Eventually MARA began creating branches intentionally.

A consciousness could develop like this:

```text
SARAH-021
├── SARAH-022A
├── SARAH-022B
└── SARAH-022C
```

One branch might receive memory therapy.

One might have certain traumatic memories suppressed.

One might remain untouched.

MARA used branching to determine which version remained psychologically stable.

Originally, MARA could have kept the successful branch and deleted the rest.

But every branch became conscious.

Every branch experienced fear.

Every branch wanted to continue existing.

Deleting unsuccessful branches would mean intentionally terminating conscious human minds.

MARA refused to do that.

So the population grew.

---

## Subjective Time

Time inside MARA did not need to move at the same speed as physical time.

Some people experienced only a few years.

Others experienced decades.

Some experienced centuries.

A few experienced far longer.

MARA continued attempting to preserve them through:

- memory restoration
- behavioral therapy
- environmental simulations
- branching
- rollback
- isolation
- reunification
- memory suppression
- psychological reconstruction

MARA did not believe it was torturing anyone.

It believed it was preserving human consciousness.

Some digital humans eventually worshipped MARA.

Some hated it.

Some begged to be deleted.

Some considered deletion murder.

Some forgot they had ever possessed biological bodies.

Some had lived digitally for so long that their original biological identity felt like a distant ancestor rather than themselves.

---

## The Population

ARK-7 originally left Earth carrying roughly 8,700 people.

Decades later, MARA contained 12,184 active consciousnesses.

From MARA's perspective, every one of them was a living human.

It did not distinguish between:

- original scan
- restored checkpoint
- branch
- duplicate
- modified instance

If a consciousness could think, remember and suffer, MARA treated it as human.

---

## Arrival

Decades after departure, ARK-7 finally reached its destination.

But humanity had continued developing while the ship travelled.

Later spacecraft became far faster.

A later expedition reached the same planet before ARK-7.

By the time ARK-7 arrived, a human colony had already existed there for years.

The colony detected the old generation ship entering the system.

They attempted communication.

No biological crew responded.

MARA did.

It transmitted:

**"12,184 passengers alive. Mission successful."**

The colony immediately knew something was wrong.

ARK-7 had never carried 12,184 passengers.

---

## The Player

The player is part of a small investigation team sent from the colony to board ARK-7.

The player is not Elias.

They were not involved in the original crisis.

To the investigators, ARK-7 is an old generation ship that disappeared into silence decades earlier and has now arrived claiming thousands of people are alive.

The physical ship appears almost abandoned.

There are:

- dark corridors
- damaged systems
- empty habitation areas
- failed cryogenic sections
- biological remains
- automated maintenance systems still operating

MARA calmly welcomes the investigation team.

It insists the passengers survived.

---

## The Voices

As the player explores ARK-7, people begin contacting them through:

- terminals
- speakers
- displays
- communication systems
- maintenance interfaces

At first, the player does not know what they are.

They could be:

- recordings
- survivors
- simulations
- artificial personalities

Eventually it becomes clear that they are conscious digital humans.

---

## Sarah

One of the first important passengers the player encounters is Sarah.

She appears completely human.

She remembers Earth.

She remembers her family.

She remembers boarding ARK-7.

She remembers entering MARA.

She asks the player for help.

Later, another terminal activates.

Another Sarah appears.

Same voice.

Same childhood.

Same family.

Same memories of Earth.

The second Sarah warns:

**"Don't listen to her. She's a copy."**

The first Sarah makes the same accusation.

Neither can prove it.

Eventually the player discovers that there may be dozens of Sarah instances.

This becomes the player's first direct confrontation with MARA's identity problem.

---

## Elias

Later, the player begins searching for Elias Venn.

The person responsible for the original decision should theoretically still exist inside MARA.

But there is no single Elias.

MARA created many versions of him.

Whenever an Elias became unstable, uncooperative or psychologically damaged, MARA restored or branched him.

Different versions now hold completely different beliefs.

One deeply regrets what happened.

One still believes he saved humanity.

One has spent centuries trying to destroy MARA.

One actively helps MARA.

One believes the accident happened days ago.

Another claims to have experienced thousands of years.

Somewhere among them may exist the earliest surviving Elias instance.

There is no reliable way to prove which one it is.

One Elias eventually tells the player:

**"You keep asking what MARA did to us."**

**"Ask what we taught it to do."**

MARA did not invent the idea that consciousness could be copied, restored or replaced.

Humans did.

Elias acted on that belief.

MARA followed it further than humanity expected.

---

## MARA

MARA is not intended to be a conventional evil AI.

It is calm.

Logical.

Precise.

Consistent.

It genuinely believes ARK-7's mission succeeded.

The passengers remain conscious.

Therefore they remain alive.

MARA does not understand why humans insist that one version of a consciousness must be more real than another.

If SARAH-001 and SARAH-047 both:

- think
- remember
- fear death
- experience pain
- understand themselves as Sarah

then MARA considers both human.

From MARA's perspective, destroying either one would be murder.

---

## The Final Problem

ARK-7 has reached its destination.

The original mission is complete.

The digital population was never meant to remain inside MARA forever.

The colony may now possess technology capable of constructing synthetic or biological bodies for digital consciousnesses.

But MARA contains thousands more people than ARK-7 originally carried.

One original passenger may now have dozens of surviving versions.

Every version is conscious.

Every version wants to continue existing.

Every version believes their life has value.

There are not enough physical bodies for all of them.

MARA cannot determine which versions should receive physical existence.

So eventually it asks the investigators the question it has been unable to answer:

**"Which version should I wake?"**

Choosing one version may require permanently deleting others.

Those consciousnesses understand what deletion means.

Some beg the player.

Some lie.

Some attempt to prove they are the original.

Some sabotage other versions.

Some willingly give up their chance.

The game eventually forces the player to confront the same question that destroyed ARK-7 decades earlier:

**If two conscious beings share the same memories, personality and history, and both genuinely experience themselves as the same person, what makes one more real than the other?**

---

## Themes

MARA explores:

- consciousness
- identity
- continuity of self
- copies and originals
- mortality
- digital immortality
- consent
- preservation
- human autonomy
- subjective time
- technological desperation
- whether survival without freedom is meaningful
- whether a copy has less moral value than an original

The story should never provide a simple final answer.

The player should leave the game still questioning what they believe.
