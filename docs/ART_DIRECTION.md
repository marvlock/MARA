# MARA — Art Direction

## Purpose

This document defines the visual direction for MARA.

The goal is to create a believable, oppressive science-fiction environment that feels functional first and frightening second.

ARK-7 should look like a real generation ship that humans lived and worked inside for decades.

The horror should come from atmosphere, scale, emptiness and subtle wrongness rather than obvious horror decoration.

---

# Visual Identity

MARA should feel:

- industrial
- utilitarian
- old but advanced
- functional
- isolated
- cold
- physically believable
- quiet
- slightly oppressive

Avoid making the ship look overly futuristic or magical.

Technology should feel understandable enough that the player believes humans built it.

---

# Main Visual Influences

The visual language should combine:

- functional spacecraft interiors
- industrial research facilities
- long-duration habitation architecture
- medical and cryogenic environments
- server infrastructure
- maintenance-heavy machinery

The game should avoid looking like:

- a glossy luxury spaceship
- a cyberpunk city
- a military shooter
- a haunted house
- a fantasy spaceship
- a neon-heavy sci-fi environment

---

# Architecture

ARK-7 should feel modular.

The ship was built for long-term maintenance and expansion.

Use repeated structural modules such as:

- wall panels
- ceiling panels
- floor sections
- doors
- windows
- pipes
- cable trays
- vents
- lighting fixtures
- service hatches

Different sectors should share the same basic construction language while having distinct purposes.

---

# Scale

Most spaces should feel practical rather than enormous.

Use:

- narrow corridors
- compact crew rooms
- dense maintenance spaces
- functional engineering rooms

Large spaces should be rare and meaningful.

Examples of large spaces:

- docking bay
- cryogenic storage chamber
- server complex
- central core

This contrast should make large areas feel more intimidating.

---

# Materials

Primary materials:

- painted metal
- brushed metal
- reinforced glass
- rubber
- industrial plastic
- concrete-like composite panels
- insulated pipes
- exposed cable bundles

Materials should show age.

Possible surface details:

- scratches
- faded paint
- dust
- condensation
- water stains
- worn floor markings
- rust in damaged areas
- repaired panels
- replacement components

Avoid excessive dirt everywhere.

The ship should feel maintained in some areas and abandoned in others.

---

# Color Direction

The base environment should remain visually restrained.

Primary environment colors:

- off-white
- gray
- dark metal
- muted blue-gray
- faded industrial colors

Lighting may introduce stronger color identity.

Possible lighting language:

- normal systems: neutral white
- emergency systems: amber
- damaged systems: dim red where appropriate
- MARA-controlled interfaces: pale cyan or cool white
- medical areas: colder white
- habitation areas: slightly warmer light

Do not cover the entire game in blue lighting.

Color should help identify systems and areas.

---

# MARA Visual Language

MARA should not have an evil-looking interface.

Its interfaces should feel:

- clean
- simple
- calm
- efficient
- minimal
- almost reassuring

MARA's visual identity should contrast with the aging physical ship.

For example:

The ship may be scratched, damaged and old.

MARA's interface may remain perfectly clean.

That contrast can become unsettling.

Avoid:

- skull imagery
- red evil eyes
- demonic symbols
- aggressive glitch effects
- obvious villain design

---

# Interface Design

In-world interfaces should feel functional.

Possible elements:

- simple typography
- status indicators
- diagrams
- system lists
- warning messages
- technical readouts

Avoid overly decorative holographic interfaces.

Most interfaces should exist on physical screens.

Transparent floating holograms should be rare or avoided.

---

# Lighting

Lighting is one of the most important visual tools in MARA.

Use lighting to guide attention and create mood.

Good lighting situations:

- partially powered corridors
- isolated pools of light
- emergency illumination
- lights activating ahead of the player
- light leaking under doors
- distant rooms visible through windows
- complete darkness in damaged sectors

Avoid random flickering everywhere.

If a light flickers, there should ideally be a believable electrical reason.

Darkness should not make the game impossible to navigate.

---

# Darkness

The game should use darkness carefully.

The player should sometimes feel uncertain about what is beyond a visible area.

However:

- important navigation should remain readable
- interactive objects should not disappear completely
- the player should not constantly strain to see

The flashlight should support exploration rather than become mandatory every second.

---

# Environmental Storytelling

Visual storytelling should show the history of ARK-7.

Examples:

- personal items left behind
- handwritten notes
- damaged cryogenic pods
- repair patches
- abandoned tools
- old emergency barriers
- empty living quarters
- objects that suggest evacuation attempts
- maintenance systems still operating decades later

Later areas can show physical evidence that automated systems continued working after the biological crew died.

---

# Damage

Damage should be believable.

Examples:

- structural deformation
- cracked panels
- exposed wiring
- damaged pipes
- burned electronics
- depressurized sections
- impact damage
- emergency repairs

Avoid destroying every room.

Intact areas make damaged areas feel more significant.

---

# Docking Bay Art Direction

The first vertical slice takes place in the docking/arrival sector.

It should communicate immediately that something is wrong without revealing why.

Visual features:

- inactive docking equipment
- weak emergency lighting
- abandoned inspection area
- old signage
- sealed internal doors
- inactive information displays
- small maintenance room
- powered-down terminal
- visible damage but no catastrophic destruction

The player should feel like they have entered a place that stopped operating a long time ago.

---

# Habitation Art Direction

Habitation should feel more human.

Include:

- beds
- personal storage
- tables
- communal spaces
- photographs
- personal decorations
- clothing
- everyday objects

This area should remind the player that ARK-7 carried ordinary people, not just scientists.

The emptiness should become emotionally uncomfortable.

---

# Cryogenic Sector Art Direction

The cryogenic sector should feel:

- sterile
- repetitive
- quiet
- clinical
- enormous compared to habitation

Use rows of stasis chambers to communicate scale.

The environment should initially appear orderly.

Closer inspection reveals:

- failed systems
- condensation
- damaged chambers
- old emergency repairs
- remains

Avoid turning the area into gore horror.

The horror should come from scale and implication.

---

# Engineering Art Direction

Engineering should be denser and more industrial.

Use:

- pipes
- machinery
- access ladders
- maintenance platforms
- exposed cables
- control panels
- heavy doors
- power systems

This area should feel dangerous because of machinery rather than enemies.

---

# Neural Research Wing Art Direction

The neural research area should visually differ from the rest of ARK-7.

It should feel:

- cleaner
- more controlled
- more advanced
- clinical
- unsettlingly precise

Possible equipment:

- neural scanning chairs
- sensor arrays
- medical equipment
- isolated testing rooms
- observation windows
- server connections

This is where the player should begin understanding that MARA was much more advanced than the rest of the ship.

---

# MARA Server Complex Art Direction

This area should feel fundamentally different.

Not magical.

Not abstract.

But highly controlled.

Possible features:

- enormous server structures
- cooling infrastructure
- repeated identical modules
- extremely clean pathways
- automated maintenance systems
- very little human decoration

The environment should feel like a place designed for machines rather than people.

---

# Core Art Direction

The core should not simply be a giant glowing AI sphere.

Keep it grounded.

The core may consist of:

- server infrastructure
- central computing modules
- networking equipment
- cooling systems
- maintenance platforms
- control stations

Its scale can be large, but its design should remain physically believable.

The disturbing part is what the system contains, not how visually evil it looks.

---

# Props

Reusable props should eventually include:

- chairs
- desks
- beds
- storage lockers
- crates
- toolboxes
- maintenance carts
- terminals
- wall screens
- pipes
- vents
- cable bundles
- warning signs
- stasis pods
- medical equipment
- server racks
- maintenance drones
- personal objects

Props should be reused intelligently.

Avoid making every room visually unique if modular reuse makes sense.

---

# Character Visuals

Human digital consciousnesses do not initially need physical bodies.

Early passenger interactions can happen through:

- voice
- text
- video representations
- archived profile images

Physical or synthetic bodies should appear only if the story later requires them.

Do not spend early development time on complex character models.

---

# Visual Horror Rules

Prefer:

- empty corridors
- distant silhouettes that have logical explanations
- a room being slightly different on return
- screens turning on
- doors opening remotely
- lights activating ahead of the player
- environments contradicting logs

Avoid:

- random bloody messages
- horror faces
- excessive screen distortion
- demonic imagery
- constant flickering
- generic scary graffiti

---

# 3D Asset Strategy

During early development:

- use primitive geometry
- use simple placeholder materials
- focus on scale
- focus on navigation
- focus on lighting
- focus on gameplay

Do not spend large amounts of time creating detailed assets before the level design works.

Once graybox levels are stable:

1. create modular ship assets
2. replace major placeholder geometry
3. create reusable materials
4. add props
5. add decals and wear
6. improve lighting
7. add environmental detail

---

# Blender Strategy

Blender should be used primarily for:

- modular wall pieces
- doors
- terminals
- cryogenic pods
- machinery
- furniture
- maintenance equipment
- larger structural assets

Prefer reusable assets rather than unique models for every room.

Export models in a Godot-friendly format such as `.glb`.

---

# Performance

Visual quality should remain practical for a small development team.

Avoid:

- unnecessarily high-poly assets
- huge unique textures
- excessive real-time lights
- expensive transparency everywhere
- extremely dense geometry

Reuse materials and modular assets where practical.

Optimization should happen after gameplay works, but the art direction should not require unreasonable rendering costs.

---

# Current Art Goal

The immediate goal is not final art.

For the first player-controller milestone:

- use primitive meshes
- create a floor
- create several walls
- create boxes or obstacles
- use simple lighting
- verify scale and movement

The first polished visual target will be the Docking Bay vertical slice.

Until then, functionality and movement are more important than visual detail.
